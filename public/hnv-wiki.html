Sūtras 1–16: The Varṇa-krama (Alphabet)

Sūtra 1nārāyaṇād udbhūto 'yaṃ varṇa-kramaḥ
nārāyaṇāt — from Lord Nārāyaṇa; udbhūtaḥ — appeared; ayam — this; varṇa-kramaḥ — series of phonemes (the Sanskrit alphabet).
This series of varṇas (the Sanskrit alphabet) appeared from Nārāyaṇa.
Sūtra 2tatrādau caturdaśa sarveśvarāḥ
tatra — there (in the alphabet); ādau — in the beginning; caturdaśa — fourteen; sarveśvarāḥ — sarveśvaras (vowels).
The first fourteen varṇas of the alphabet are called sarveśvaras. (a ā i ī u ū ṛ ṝ ḷ ḹ e ai o au)
Sūtra 3daśa daśāvatārāḥ
daśa — ten; daśāvatārāḥ — daśāvatāras.
The first ten varṇas of the alphabet are called daśāvatāras. (a ā i ī u ū ṛ ṝ ḷ ḹ)
Sūtra 4teṣāṃ dvau dvāv ekātmakau
teṣām — among them (the daśāvatāras); dvau dvau — every two varṇas; ekātmakau — are two ekātmakas (of the same nature).
Among the daśāvatāras, both varṇas of each consecutive pair are called ekātmakas. (a/ā, i/ī, u/ū, ṛ/ṝ, ḷ/ḹ)
Sūtra 5pūrvo vāmanaḥ
pūrvaḥ — the first one; vāmanaḥ — vāmana (short vowel).
The first varṇa in each pair of ekātmakas is called vāmana. (a i u ṛ ḷ — Pāṇinian: hrasva)
Sūtra 6paras trivikramaḥ
paraḥ — the other one; trivikramaḥ — trivikrama (long vowel).
The latter varṇa in each pair of ekātmakas is called trivikrama. (ā ī ū ṝ ḹ — Pāṇinian: dīrgha)
Sūtra 7a-ā-varjitāḥ sarveśvarā īśvarāḥ
a-ā-varjitāḥ — excluding the varṇas a and ā; sarveśvarāḥ — sarveśvaras; īśvarāḥ — īśvaras.
The sarveśvaras excluding a and ā are called īśvaras. (i ī u ū ṛ ṝ ḷ ḹ e ai o au)
Sūtra 8daśāvatārā īśāḥ
daśāvatārāḥ — daśāvatāras; īśāḥ — īśas.
The daśāvatāras that are also īśvaras are called īśas. (i ī u ū ṛ ṝ ḷ ḹ)
Sūtra 9a-ā-i-ī-u-ū anantāḥ
a-ā-i-ī-u-ū — the varṇas a, ā, i, ī, u, and ū; anantāḥ — anantas.
A, ā, i, ī, u, and ū are called anantas.
Sūtra 10i-ī-u-ū catuḥ-sanāḥ
i-ī-u-ū — the varṇas i, ī, u, and ū; catuḥ-sanāḥ — catuḥsanas.
I, ī, u, and ū are called catuḥsanas.
Sūtra 11u-ū-ṛ-ṝ catur-bhujāḥ
u-ū-ṛ-ṝ — the varṇas u, ū, ṛ, and ṝ; catur-bhujāḥ — caturbhujas.
U, ū, ṛ, and ṝ are called caturbhujas.
Sūtra 12e-ai-o-au catur-vyūhāḥ
e-ai-o-au — the varṇas e, ai, o, and au; catur-vyūhāḥ — caturvyūhas (diphthongs).
E, ai, o, and au are called caturvyūhas. (Pāṇinian: sandhyakṣaras. All are trivikramas.)
Sūtra 13aṃ iti viṣṇu-cakram
aṃ — the varṇa ṃ; iti — thus; viṣṇu-cakram — viṣṇucakra.
Aṃ is called viṣṇucakra. (Pāṇinian: anusvāra)
Sūtra 14a̐ iti viṣṇu-cāpaḥ
a̐ — the varṇa a̐ (anunāsika); iti — thus; viṣṇu-cāpaḥ — viṣṇucāpa.
A̐ is called viṣṇucāpa. (Pāṇinian: anunāsika. Pronounced in the nose.)
Sūtra 15aḥ iti viṣṇu-sargaḥ
aḥ — the varṇa ḥ; iti — thus; viṣṇu-sargaḥ — viṣṇusarga.
Aḥ is called viṣṇusarga. (Pāṇinian: visarga)
Sūtra 16kādayo viṣṇu-janāḥ
ka-ādayaḥ — the varṇas beginning with ka; viṣṇu-janāḥ — viṣṇujanas (consonants).
All the varṇas from ka onward are called viṣṇujanas. (Pāṇinian: vyañjanas / hal-s. Note: kṣa is the combination of ka and ṣa, not a separate viṣṇujana.)

Sūtras 17–30: Consonant Classifications

Sūtra 17te māntāḥ pañca pañca viṣṇu-vargāḥ
te — the viṣṇujanas; māntāḥ — ending with the varṇa ma; pañca pañca — in five groups of five; viṣṇu-vargāḥ — viṣṇuvargas.
The viṣṇujanas from ka to ma, arranged in five groups of five, are called viṣṇuvargas. (Pāṇinian: vargas)
Sūtra 18ka-ca-ṭa-ta-pā hari-kamalāni
ka-ca-ṭa-ta-pāḥ — the viṣṇujanas ka, ca, ṭa, ta, and pa; hari-kamalāni — harikamalas.
Ka, ca, ṭa, ta, and pa are called harikamalas. (The lotus of Hari — voiceless unaspirated stops)
Sūtra 19kha-cha-ṭha-tha-phā hari-khaḍgāḥ
kha-cha-ṭha-tha-phāḥ — the viṣṇujanas kha, cha, ṭha, tha, and pha; hari-khaḍgāḥ — harikhaḍgas.
Kha, cha, ṭha, tha, and pha are called harikhaḍgas. (The sword of Hari — voiceless aspirated stops)
Sūtra 20ga-ja-ḍa-da-bā hari-gadāḥ
ga-ja-ḍa-da-bāḥ — the viṣṇujanas ga, ja, ḍa, da, and ba; hari-gadāḥ — harigadās.
Ga, ja, ḍa, da, and ba are called harigadās. (The club of Hari — voiced unaspirated stops)
Sūtra 21gha-jha-ḍha-dha-bhā hari-ghoṣāḥ
gha-jha-ḍha-dha-bhāḥ — the viṣṇujanas gha, jha, ḍha, dha, and bha; hari-ghoṣāḥ — harighoṣas.
Gha, jha, ḍha, dha, and bha are called harighoṣas. (The conchshell of Hari — voiced aspirated stops)
Sūtra 22ṅa-ña-ṇa-na-mā hari-veṇavaḥ
ṅa-ña-ṇa-na-māḥ — the viṣṇujanas ṅa, ña, ṇa, na, and ma; hari-veṇavaḥ — hariveṇus.
Ṅa, ña, ṇa, na, and ma are called hariveṇus. (The flute of Hari — nasals. Pronounced using both mouth and nose.)
Sūtra 23ta etad-varjitā viṣṇu-dāsāḥ
te — they (the viṣṇuvargas); etat-varjitāḥ — excluding these (the hariveṇus); viṣṇu-dāsāḥ — viṣṇudāsas.
The viṣṇuvargas excluding the hariveṇus are called viṣṇudāsas. (i.e., the 20 stops: ka–bha)
Sūtra 24ya-ra-la-vā hari-mitrāṇi
ya-ra-la-vāḥ — the viṣṇujanas ya, ra, la, and va; hari-mitrāṇi — harimitras.
Ya, ra, la, and va are called harimitras. (The friends of Hari — semivowels)
Sūtra 25śa-ṣa-sa-hā hari-gotrāṇi
śa-ṣa-sa-hāḥ — the viṣṇujanas śa, ṣa, sa, and ha; hari-gotrāṇi — harigotras.
Śa, ṣa, sa, and ha are called harigotras. (The family of Hari — sibilants + ha)
Sūtra 26śa-ṣa-sāḥ śaurayaḥ
śa-ṣa-sāḥ — the viṣṇujanas śa, ṣa, and sa; śaurayaḥ — śauris.
Śa, ṣa, and sa are called śauris. (The sibilants)
Sūtra 27viṣṇudāsa-harigotrāṇi vaiṣṇavāḥ
viṣṇudāsa-harigotrāṇi — the viṣṇudāsas and harigotras; vaiṣṇavāḥ — vaiṣṇavas.
The viṣṇudāsas and the harigotras are called vaiṣṇavas.
Sūtra 28harigadā-harighoṣa-hariveṇu-harimitrāṇi haś ca gopālāḥ
harigadā-harighoṣa-hariveṇu-harimitrāṇi — the harigadās, harighoṣas, hariveṇus, and harimitras; haḥ — the varṇa ha; ca — and; gopālāḥ — gopālas.
The harigadās, harighoṣas, hariveṇus, harimitras, and ha are called gopālas. (The soft/voiced consonants)
Sūtra 29yādavā anye
yādavāḥ — yādavas; anye — the other viṣṇujanas.
All viṣṇujanas other than gopālas are called yādavas. (The hard/voiceless consonants)
Sūtra 30śauri-varjitās tu sātvatāḥ
śauri-varjitāḥ — excluding the śauris; tu — but; sātvatāḥ — sātvatas.
The yādavas excluding the śauris are called sātvatas.

Sūtras 31–40: Prosody, Terminology & Operations

Sūtra 31vāmano laghuḥ
vāmanaḥ — a vāmana (short vowel); laghuḥ — laghu (prosodically light).
A vāmana is laghu.
Sūtra 32trivikramo guruḥ
trivikramaḥ — a trivikrama (long vowel); guruḥ — guru (prosodically heavy).
A trivikrama is guru.
Sūtra 33sat-saṅgāt pūrvo vāmano 'pi guruḥ
sat-saṅgāt — a sat-saṅga (consonant cluster); pūrvaḥ — before; vāmanaḥ — a vāmana; api — also; guruḥ — guru.
A vāmana that immediately precedes a sat-saṅga is also guru.
Sūtra 34mithaḥ-saṃlagno viṣṇujanaḥ sat-saṅga-saṃjñaḥ
mithaḥ — with each other; saṃlagnaḥ — conjoined; viṣṇujanaḥ — viṣṇujanas; sat-saṅga-saṃjñaḥ — called sat-saṅga.
The combination of viṣṇujanas conjoined with each other is called a sat-saṅga.
Sūtra 35varṇa-svarūpe rāmaḥ
varṇa-svarūpe — when expressing a varṇa itself; rāmaḥ — rāma.
The word rāma is used to designate a single varṇa. (e.g., a-rāma = the varṇa a)
Sūtra 36tad-ādi-dvaye dvayam
tat — that; ādi — beginning with; dvaye — when expressing a pair; dvayam — dvaya.
The word dvaya is used to designate the pair of varṇas beginning with the varṇa mentioned. (e.g., i-dvaya = i and ī)
Sūtra 37ādeśo viriñciḥ
ādeśaḥ — substitution; viriñciḥ — viriñci.
Substitution is called viriñci. (Pāṇinian: ādeśa)
Sūtra 38āgamo viṣṇuḥ
āgamaḥ — insertion/augment; viṣṇuḥ — viṣṇu.
Insertion is called viṣṇu. (Pāṇinian: āgama)
Sūtra 39lopo haraḥ
lopaḥ — deletion; haraḥ — hara.
Deletion is called hara. (Pāṇinian: lopa)
Sūtra 40sūtrāṇi ṣaḍ-vidhāni
sūtrāṇi — sūtras; ṣaḍ-vidhāni — six kinds.
There are six kinds of sūtras.

Sūtra 40 (sūtrāṇi ṣaḍ-vidhāni) states there are six kinds of sūtras. The six types are: (1) saṃjñā (definition), (2) paribhāṣā (interpretive rule), (3) vidhi (operational rule), (4) niyama (restriction), (5) atideśa (extension by analogy), (6) adhikāra (governing rule). Chapter 1B — Sandhi (Sūtras 41–86)

Phonetic combinations: vowel sandhi, consonant sandhi, visarga sandhi

What is Sandhi?

Sandhi (from sam + dhā = "putting together") refers to the phonetic changes that occur at word boundaries or within words when sounds meet. HNV divides sandhi into three types:

TypeSūtrasHNV NameDescription
Vowel Sandhi42–60Sarveśvara-sandhiChanges when two vowels meet
Consonant Sandhi61–78Viṣṇujana-sandhiChanges at consonant junctions
Visarga Sandhi79–86Viṣṇusarga-sandhiChanges involving ḥ (visarga)

Vowel Sandhi (Sūtras 42–60)

Sūtra 42daśāvatāra ekātmake militvā trivikramaḥ
daśāvatāraḥ — a daśāvatāra; ekātmake — when an ekātmaka follows; militvā — joining; trivikramaḥ — trivikrama.

When a-dvaya is followed by i-dvaya, it joins with i-dvaya and becomes e.

Sūtra 45a-dvayasya ṛ-dvaye ar

Sūtra 46a-dvayasya e-ai-sthāne ai

Sūtra 47a-dvayasya o-au-sthāne au

Sūtra 49o-dvaye au

Sūtra 50i-dvayam eva yaḥ sarveśvare
i-dvayam — i-dvaya (i or ī); eva — only; yaḥ — ya-rāma; sarveśvare — when a sarveśvara follows.

Sūtra 61viṣṇudāso viṣṇupadānte harighoṣe ca harigadā

Exam Q&A — Chapters 1 & 2

1. What are the 4 types of nāma in HNV?
Puruṣottama (masculine), Lakṣmī (feminine), Brahma (neuter), Avyaya (indeclinable). These replace the Pāṇinian terms puṃliṅga, strīliṅga, napuṃsakaliṅga, avyaya.
2. What is the difference between govinda and vṛṣṇīndra?
Govinda (= guṇa): i→e, u→o, ṛ→ar, ḷ→al. Vṛṣṇīndra (= vṛddhi): a→ā, i→ai, u→au, ṛ→ār, e→ai, o→au. Govinda is applied when [j]as, vṛṣṇis, or buddha follows a hari. Vṛṣṇīndra is applied to sakhi before pāṇḍavas (except s[u]).
3. Give the 8 cases of kṛṣṇa in singular.
kṛṣṇaḥ, kṛṣṇam, kṛṣṇena, kṛṣṇāya, kṛṣṇāt, kṛṣṇasya, kṛṣṇe, kṛṣṇa (voc.)
4. Why does rāma have rāmeṇa (not rāmena) in tṛtīyā sg.?
Sūtra 111: n → ṇ after r/ṣ/ṛ-dvaya in the same viṣṇupada. In rāmeṇa, the r of rāma causes n of ina to become ṇ. Similarly rāmāṇām (gen. pl.) and rāmeṣu (loc. pl.).
5. What is the difference between pitṛ and kartṛ declension?
pitṛ-type: nom. sg. = pitā (no vṛṣṇīndra in nom. du./pl.) → pitarau, pitaraḥ. kartṛ-type (tṛl/tṛn agent nouns): nom. sg. = kartā, nom. du. = kartārau, nom. pl. = kartāraḥ (vṛṣṇīndra by Sūtra 139). Both have pituḥ/kartuḥ in gen./abl. sg.
6. How does gopī differ from bhakti in the 4th case singular?
gopī: always ā[p] (nityam) → gopyai (only one form). bhakti: optionally ā[p] → bhaktyai OR bhaktaye (two forms). This is because Sūtra 150 says ā[p] is optional (vā) after a hari in feminine, but always (nityam) after a gopī.
7. Give the nom./acc./voc. of gokula in all three numbers.
Sg.: gokulam / gokulam / gokula. Du.: gokule / gokule / gokule. Pl.: gokulāni / gokulāni / gokulāni. (Nom. = Acc. = Voc. in neuter — universal rule.)
8. What is the difference between dadhi and vāri declension?
Both are neuter i-stems. dadhi: i→an before [ṭ]ā onwards (Sūtra 168), then a optionally deleted (Sūtra 170) → dadhnā, dadhne, etc. Optional voc. sg.: dadhe or dadhi. vāri: n→ṇ throughout (ṇatva by Sūtra 111 because ā+r precedes) → vāriṇā, vāriṇe, etc. No govinda option in voc.
9. What are the strong cases (kṛṣṇasthāna) in consonant-stem declension?
The kṛṣṇasthānas are: the pāṇḍavas (s[u], au, [j]as, am, au) except when in brahma-liṅga, and [ś]i. In strong cases, the uddhava of n-ending words takes trivikrama (e.g., rājān-, bhagavant-). In weak cases (yadus), the a of -an is deleted.
10. Give the nom. sg. of: bhagavat, rājan, śārṅgin, kaṃsa-han, pathin.
bhagavān (trivikrama+n[um]+s[u] deleted+sat-saṅga deleted), rājā (trivikrama+s[u] deleted+n deleted), śārṅgī (trivikrama+s[u] deleted+n deleted), kaṃsa-hā (trivikrama+s[u] deleted+n deleted), panthāḥ (i→ā+n[uk]+s[u]).
11. What is the tṛtīyā sg. of: rājan, ātman, śvan, yuvan, maghavan?
rājñā (a deleted, j+ñ=jñ), ātmanā (a NOT deleted — ma-sat-saṅga restriction), śunā (va→u), yūnā (va→u, u+u→ū), maghonā (va→u, a+u→o).
12. How does the numeral pañcan differ from tri in declension?
tri: always plural, vācya-liṅga (adjectival), masc. only (fem. = tiṣṛ). Nom./acc. pl. = trayaḥ/trīn. pañcan: always plural, same form in all 3 genders. Nom./acc. pl. = pañca (mahāhara of [j]as and [ś]as). Gen. pl.: trayāṇām vs. pañcānām.
13. What is the difference between tad, etad, and idam?
tad (he/she/it/that) — distal demonstrative; nom. sg. masc. = saḥ. etad (this) — proximate demonstrative; nom. sg. masc. = eṣaḥ. idam (this, near) — near demonstrative; nom. sg. masc. = ayam; uses anena (not tena) in tṛtīyā sg. All three have no vocative.
14. Give the gen. sg. of: tad (M), idam (M), asmad, yuṣmad.
tasya, asya, mama (viriñci: me), tava (viriñci: te).
15. What is the key difference between sarva-type and kṛṣṇa-type in caturthī sg.?
kṛṣṇa-type: caturthī sg. = kṛṣṇāya (ending -āya). sarva-type: caturthī sg. = sarvasmai (ending -smai). Similarly pañcamī: -āt vs. -smāt; saptamī: -e vs. -smin. This is the key diagnostic for sarva-type pronouns.
16. What is buddha and when does it disappear?
Buddha is the name for s[u] in the sambodhana (vocative) case (Sūtra 109). It disappears (Sūtra 110) after e-rāma, o-rāma, or vāmana. Examples: he hare (not hariḥ), he viṣṇo (not viṣṇuḥ), he kṛṣṇa (not kṛṣṇaḥ). It does NOT disappear after ā (rādhā → rādhe, not rādhā).
17. What is the difference between mahāhara and hara?
Hara (= lopa) = simple deletion. Mahāhara = complete deletion that prevents sthāni-vat (the deleted element is not treated as still present). Mahāhara blocks govinda and other operations that would be triggered by the deleted element. Example: pañcan + [j]as → (126) pañca (mahāhara of [j]as prevents govinda).
18. Give the full declension of go in singular.
gauḥ, gām, gavā, gave, goḥ, goḥ, gavi, gauḥ (voc. — s[u] not deleted because vidhāna-sāmarthyāt).
19. What is the gen. pl. of: kṛṣṇa, hari, viṣṇu, pitṛ, rādhā, gopī?
kṛṣṇānām, harīṇām, viṣṇūnām, pitṝṇām, rādhānām, gopīnām.
20. What is the loc. sg. of: rājan, ātman, brahman (neut.), ahan?
rājñi or rājani (optional a-deletion), ātmani (a not deleted), brahmaṇi, ahni or ahani (optional a-deletion).
